# A concrete pawn reply can disprove a terminal claim

After White plays Qg6–c6, a black king on a7 has no king move. With a black pawn on a6 it can still play a6–a5. Remove that one pawn and the position is stalemate. The sample tries `playing`, `draw` and `won` with an identical move and board for each claim, then mirrors the pair for Black to move. [Positions and native replies](../../exploration/sole-pawn-v1/controls/native.json)

The candidate derives one possible ordinary pawn move in each of three directions on a packed copy of the successor. The compiled helper checks whether at least one of those moves leaves the defending king safe. Empty diagonal captures, off-board moves, friendly captures, pawn advances into occupied squares and attempts to capture the enemy king cannot satisfy the matching pattern. The predicate treats a promotion as an occupied destination of the defending color for king safety. It supplies no promotion move to a game client. [Policy source](policy.mjs)

The independent evidence checked the actual source and destination changed by each projected move on 3,246 positions. Of 3,468 concrete proposed moves, 287 left their own king attacked. The predicate found a legal reply on 1,755 of 1,799 positions with one or more legal pawn moves. On 18 it selected a different pawn that could not legally move. On 26, an initial two-square move or en passant was required. The full move list remains native truth on all 44. The archived proof also runs the emitted helper under an independent Rules evaluator. [Proof data](proof.json) · [Reproduction](qualify.mjs)

The paired variant selects from both ends in each direction. On the same 3,246 boards this finds 14 additional legal replies. Four single-step or capture witnesses still fall between the selected pawns, and 26 require a double advance or en passant. In the complete match histories, the extra witness correctly denies Black's win claim after ...Nb4–d3 in seed 503, where e2xd3 is a legal reply. All 37 honest sequences complete.
